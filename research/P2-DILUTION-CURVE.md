# P2 — Dilution-Response Curve

## Research question

Does each Diamond Brew have a reproducible dilution region that maximizes clarity or preference?

## Why this has high value

Dilution requires no additional pressure-processing batches and directly affects customer experience, Coffee Fest sampling, vial serving instructions, and wholesale specifications.

## Primary hypothesis

Preference and perceived clarity will form a coffee-specific optimum rather than increasing monotonically with concentration.

## Sample preparation

Begin with one homogeneous, well-mixed Diamond Brew batch.

Choose a dilution series broad enough to include the current service region and the suspected clearer region. An initial series could include:

- 1:3;
- 1:4;
- 1:5;
- 1:6;
- 1:7;
- 1:8 where appropriate to the coffee and intended beverage.

Ratios must use one consistent convention throughout the experiment. State explicitly whether 1:5 means one part concentrate plus five parts water or one part concentrate in five total parts.

Use the same dilution water and service temperature for every sample. Prepare by mass rather than volume where practical.

## Serving dose, dilution ratio, and beverage size

October 15 development reports described clearer or more expressive beverages using 30 mL rather than 40 mL concentrate across several profiles, alongside exploration near 1:7 or greater dilution. These are development preferences, not controlled validation or universal serving standards.

A concentrate dose, concentrate-to-added-water ratio, and total beverage size describe related but different quantities. For a fixed concentrate and a two-component beverage, only two are independently selectable. Define ratios by mass here: 1:7 means one part concentrate plus seven parts added water. Do not translate a reported volumetric service ratio into a mass ratio without measurements; record the original convention or mark it unknown.

Use separate comparisons:

- Hold final beverage mass fixed and vary concentrate dose, adjusting added water. This tests the dose/dilution combination at one beverage size; it cannot isolate dose from concentration.
- Hold dilution ratio fixed and vary total prepared serving size. This tests serving-size/context effects while concentration remains nominally constant; use equal tasting aliquots for the initial sensory comparison.
- Hold concentrate mass fixed and vary added water for the dilution-response curve. Screen the 1:7–1:8 region, then use fine intervals only where justified.

Record concentrate mass and, when used in service, volume with measurement temperature and density conversion basis. Do not assume 30 mL equals 30 g. Evaluate coffees separately and repeat blinded comparisons before prescribing a dose or dilution.

## Extraction ratio versus serving dilution

Extraction ratio describes dry coffee to water added during brewing; serving dilution describes recovered concentrate to water added afterward. Label both explicitly. A change from, for example, 1:3.5 to 1:5 extraction is not the same treatment as adding more service water to one concentrate. A logged ratio or formula-derived target does not establish the actual weighed inputs or an adopted method change.

Use this extension with P8 in the [research roadmap](RESEARCH-ROADMAP.md):

1. Predefine extraction-ratio levels for one coffee lot, using actual weighed coffee and extraction-water masses. Start with equal dry-coffee mass; record unavoidable fill/headspace and thermal-history differences rather than assuming the apparatus remains equivalent.
2. Replicate extraction conditions across independent canner cycles/production days; randomize treatment allocation where feasible. Multiple aliquots of one brew are sensory repeats, not independent extraction replicates.
3. Recover and weigh concentrate using the same clarification procedure. Record losses, conforming usable output, and suitable calibrated TDS with sample preparation and measurement uncertainty. Formula targets or volume alone cannot establish extracted-solids yield.
4. Map a serving-dilution curve within each extraction treatment. Separately compare treatments at a common measured final TDS achievable by dilution, with the same dilution water, service temperature, and tasting aliquot size. Keep any residual water-chemistry differences explicit.
5. Use blinded, randomized tasting to compare preference and attributes at matched strength, then compare each treatment's separately selected preferred formulation. These answer different questions. Record the actual concentrate and added-water masses in both comparisons.

Estimate apparent recovered dissolved solids as recovered concentrate mass multiplied by appropriately measured TDS mass fraction, and normalize to actual dry-coffee mass. This is a method-dependent recovered-solids estimate, not complete extraction from all retained liquid or a direct chemical-composition measurement. Record the mass-balance boundary and limitations from minerals, colloids, and instrument suitability. Keep usable concentrate recovery, apparent recovered-solids yield, and conforming servings at a common strength separate; more liquid does not establish better extraction or sensory quality.

If reliable TDS matching is unavailable, report the unmatched comparison as exploratory. Do not claim an extraction-ratio advantage from a comparison confounded by final strength. Reject or flag unresolved duplicate/corrected batch records before aggregating output; use stable batch and cycle IDs in the [experiment template](../experiments/EXPERIMENT-TEMPLATE.md).

## Fine-interval follow-up

After broad screening, predefine a narrower series around a promising region—for example 1 part concentrate plus 4.75, 5.00, and 5.25 parts added water by mass. Small differences have been reported in development, but their repeatability and perceptual magnitude remain unestablished. Keep dilution-water composition fixed; test water chemistry separately under P5b.

Repeat with independently prepared samples and across production days. Limit samples per session and balance order to reduce fatigue and carryover. Confirm a selected region in a new blinded session rather than treating the best score from exploratory screening as a proven optimum.

## Independent sensory evaluation

Recruit qualified independent coffee sensory evaluators and document their relevant experience, protocol, and any commercial relationship. Evaluate preferred drinking strength, perceived flavor clarity, and independently assessed sensory quality separately; their optima may differ. Use a stated scoring framework suitable for the finished beverage and retain individual responses.

For comparison with conventional brewing, use the same coffee lot, matched measured final strength with a suitable coffee-TDS method, controlled water and service temperature, and blinded randomized presentation. Record remaining process differences; a matched-strength comparison does not isolate extraction temperature alone. If reliable strength matching is unavailable, disclose that limitation rather than claiming equivalence.

## Measurements

For each dilution:

- concentrate mass;
- dilution-water mass;
- calculated dilution and explicit mass/volume convention;
- total prepared beverage mass/volume and tasting aliquot size;
- measured °Brix or TDS if the instrument and method are appropriate;
- service temperature;
- time between preparation and tasting.

## Sensory design

Use randomized codes and change presentation order across tasters.

Rate:

- aroma intensity;
- flavor separation;
- origin-character clarity;
- roast emphasis;
- sweetness;
- acidity;
- bitterness;
- astringency;
- body;
- finish;
- integration;
- overall preference.

Also ask:

- Which sample best expresses this coffee?
- Which sample would you choose as a full serving?
- At what point does dilution become clearly excessive?

## Analysis

Plot dilution against each mean attribute and preference score.

Look for:

- a peak rather than a straight-line relationship;
- separate optima for preferred drinking strength, clarity, and assessed sensory quality;
- uncertainty and individual responses rather than only the highest mean score;
- an intensity threshold below which the coffee loses identity;
- strong differences among tasters;
- order or temperature effects.

## Decision rule

Do not define the serving ratio solely by maximum strength. Choose a service region that balances expression, preference, economics, and intended beverage format.

A single optimum should not be assumed across coffees. The useful commercial output may be a repeatable dialing procedure rather than one universal dilution.
