# P3 — Agitation Contribution

## Research question

Does controlled agitation materially change extraction, repeatability, clarification, or sensory results under the current Diamond Brew method?

## Primary hypothesis

Agitation improves slurry uniformity and reduces batch variability, but excessive agitation may increase suspended fines or clarification loss.

## Safety boundary

Never shake, move, open, vent, obstruct, or modify a pressurized cooker or canner contrary to its manufacturer instructions.

This protocol applies only to the established safe method of agitating the contained coffee mixture. If the existing procedure cannot be varied without manipulating the pressure vessel unsafely, defer this experiment until suitable equipment is available.

## Initial conditions

Compare at minimum:

- no intentional agitation beyond required handling;
- current standardized agitation procedure.

A later experiment may include a reduced-agitation condition if the first comparison shows a meaningful effect.

## Design

Use:

- the same coffee and water batches;
- the same ratio and grind;
- equivalent product containers and fill geometry;
- the same thermal hold;
- the same cooling and filtration sequence;
- randomized condition placement where possible;
- at least three independent production-day repetitions.

If multiple containers are processed in the same canner, account for position. Rotate positions across repetitions.

## Measurements

Record:

- recovered concentrate mass;
- °Brix or TDS using a documented method;
- filtration time;
- mass retained in filters;
- visible sediment or turbidity;
- repeatability across samples;
- sensory results at equal dilution and, where possible, matched strength.

## Sensory attributes

- aroma intensity;
- clarity;
- body;
- bitterness;
- astringency;
- sediment perception;
- integration;
- overall preference.

## Decision rule

Retain agitation only to the degree that it produces a repeatable benefit in recovery, uniformity, or sensory quality that exceeds its labor, safety, and filtration costs.

If agitation changes turbidity or filtration but not measured strength or sensory quality, simplify the procedure.

If the difference is inconsistent across repetitions, treat agitation as unproven rather than selecting the preferred single batch.

## Limitation

“No intentional agitation” is not “no fluid motion.” Heating produces convection, gas movement, and other internal mixing. The comparison tests the incremental effect of the intentional procedure.
