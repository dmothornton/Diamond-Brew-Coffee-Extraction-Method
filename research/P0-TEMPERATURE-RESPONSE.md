# P0 — Temperature-Response Protocol

## Research question

How do recovery, measured strength, clarification behavior, and sensory character change as direct coffee-water extraction approaches the current Diamond Brew condition of nominally 121 °C?

## Status of 121 °C

Nominal 121 °C is the defining condition of the current Diamond Brew method. It is not yet established as a natural chemical threshold. This experiment is intended to determine whether the response is continuous, nonlinear, or dominated by coupled variables.

A result below 121 °C that resembles Diamond Brew would constrain mechanistic claims. It would not automatically redefine the method.

## Competing models

1. **Continuous response:** extraction rate and thermal transformation change gradually with temperature.
2. **Nonlinear region:** one or more practically important responses accelerate strongly near the current target.
3. **Coupled-process explanation:** pressure, closure, evaporation, come-up time, or equipment differences explain much of the apparent temperature effect.
4. **Convention without threshold:** 121 °C remains a useful reproducible method definition even if no sharp transition is observed.

## Equipment requirement and safety boundary

Use only equipment designed and approved for controlled operation at every tested temperature and pressure. Never obstruct, defeat, modify, or improvise a pressure-relief system. Never move, shake, open, or vent a pressurized vessel contrary to manufacturer instructions.

If current equipment cannot safely control and directly document multiple temperature conditions, defer the comparative experiment. Do not infer product temperature solely from a dial setting without documenting that limitation.

## Staged design

### Stage A — Measurement validation at the current condition

Before constructing a temperature curve:

- document sensor type, calibration status, accuracy, and location;
- distinguish chamber, water-bath, container-surface, and coffee-slurry temperatures;
- quantify come-up time and lag between monitored locations;
- define when the hold begins;
- repeat the nominal 121 °C condition across at least three runs;
- estimate within-condition variability.

This stage determines whether differences between later temperature conditions can be resolved.

### Stage B — Broad temperature screen

Using purpose-suitable equipment, compare a broad series such as nominal 100, 110, 115, and 121 °C. These are starting points, not claimed boundaries.

Keep constant where technically possible:

- coffee lot and roast batch;
- water composition;
- grind distribution;
- coffee-to-water ratio;
- product-container geometry and fill;
- agitation rule;
- hold-start definition and hold duration;
- cooling and clarification;
- analytical sample preparation;
- final tasting strength.

Randomize run order where equipment permits. Repeat on at least three independent days.

Temperature and pressure are coupled in liquid-water systems. Record both and avoid claiming that this design isolates pressure.

### Stage C — Boundary refinement

Only if Stage B shows a repeatable nonlinear region, add closely spaced temperatures around that region. Predefine the additional conditions and decision rule before examining the new results.

## Measurements

Record:

- actual temperature traces and sensor locations;
- pressure or approved equipment operating condition;
- come-up, hold, and cooling times;
- coffee and water input masses;
- recovered concentrate and retained-liquid masses;
- filtration time and filter loss;
- calibrated TDS where methodologically valid, with dilution and sample preparation;
- pH where useful;
- turbidity or a documented visual proxy;
- sensory results at equal recipe dilution and matched final strength.

## Sensory evaluation

Use randomized three-digit codes and rotate presentation order. At minimum, evaluate:

- aroma intensity;
- origin-character clarity;
- roast emphasis;
- sweetness;
- perceived acidity;
- bitterness;
- astringency;
- body;
- cooked or processed character;
- integration;
- overall preference.

Record attribute intensity before preference.

## Interpretation

Plot each response against actual measured extraction temperature, not nominal equipment settings alone.

Evidence for a nonlinear region should require:

- a difference larger than within-condition measurement variability;
- the same directional pattern across independent days;
- no obvious alternative explanation from time, pressure, evaporation, geometry, or sample preparation;
- replication with at least one additional coffee before proposing a general boundary.

Failure to find a discontinuity supports a continuous-temperature model; it does not show that temperature is unimportant.

## Reporting requirement

Publish raw temperature traces, operating conditions, recipes, measurements, sensory responses, deviations, exclusions, and null results. Another investigator should be able to reproduce the tested conditions without relying on undocumented Sweet Caffeine knowledge.
