## Coffee-kanten

**Question:** Can a Diamond Brew agar gel retain desirable coffee expression with a useful texture at a documented formulation?

Compare two stages rather than changing all ingredients together:

1. Fix one concentrate batch, coffee loading, final water composition, sweetener, and serving temperature; screen predefined agar concentrations appropriate to the selected food-grade product's instructions. Any proposed agar range is a test condition, not an established recipe.
2. Hold a promising gel formulation fixed and vary coffee loading/dilution. Use blinded randomized samples to evaluate coffee clarity, sweetness, bitterness, aroma, texture, and overall preference separately.

Record agar identity and mass, all ingredient masses, concentrate dose and final dilution, heating/activation history, mold geometry, cooling/setting time, serving temperature, liquid separation, and usable portion recovery. Confirm a candidate with independent preparations before publishing application guidance. Agar setting, taste, or appearance does not establish shelf life or microbial safety; apply the existing [safety limits](../docs/SAFETY.md).

## Co-ferment coffee evaluation

**Question:** How do coffees sold as co-ferments express themselves through Diamond Brew, across controlled dilutions, and which preferences replicate under blinded conditions?

For one traceable lot, first test predefined dilutions using one homogeneous concentrate, fixed water and temperature, randomized codes, and balanced order. Record aroma/flavor descriptors, sourness, sweetness, bitterness, clarity, and preference separately. Repeat across independently brewed batches and evaluators. If testing expectation effects, use a separate labeled-versus-blinded comparison; keep tasting instructions neutral during the initial blind evaluation.

For comparisons with other coffees or brewing methods, document lot, roast, extraction, water, and final-strength differences. Use a suitable calibrated TDS method where matching is claimed. Unrelated lots cannot isolate co-fermentation as the cause of a sensory difference; one especially acidic or fruity sample does not establish a category-wide effect.

Keep producer-reported processing, observed sensory descriptors, and causal interpretation in separate fields. A fruit descriptor alone cannot distinguish transferred fruit compounds, microbial formation, roast effects, or expectation. Resolving those mechanisms requires traceable processing controls and suitable chemical analysis. Do not infer a percentage attributable to infusion versus fermentation from tasting alone.

**Decision:** Identify repeatable beverage preferences and questions worth testing. No preferred coffee category, validated kanten recipe, or causal co-fermentation claim is established here.
