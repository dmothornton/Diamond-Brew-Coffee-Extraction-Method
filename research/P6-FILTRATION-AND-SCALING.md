# P6 — Filtration and Scaling

## Purpose and evidence status

Reduce hands-on labor while preserving concentrate recovery and beverage quality. Finer filtration and maximum optical clarity are not goals by themselves. This document is a research plan, not an equipment specification or validated production protocol.

Development observations support investigating residual fines, bottled sediment, and whole-slurry filtration. The proposed cake mechanism remains uncertain. The scaling concepts below have not been validated for Diamond Brew; listing them does not establish suitability, adoption, or performance.

## Baseline and stage roles

The current development baseline is whole-slurry muslin filtration, gravity drainage, manual squeezing of the remainder, and secondary clarification where used. Paper filtration developed as a practical response to residual fines and bottled sediment; its necessity and sensory effects are not established. See [Development History](../docs/DEVELOPMENT-HISTORY.md).

Measure each stage independently. Keep gravity-drained and pressed fractions separate initially, then compare their individual and recombined sensory character at controlled dilution. Record whether downstream clarification saves work or simply relocates it.

The [roadmap](RESEARCH-ROADMAP.md) defines comparisons of whole slurry, decanted liquid, and controlled grounds precoat. Add original slurry, fines-enriched liquid, and liquid recombined with a characterized coarse fraction where feasible. Account for feed composition and liquid/solutes introduced by added grounds. These comparisons test whether cake structure, fines distribution, or both explain the reported behavior; they do not assume the mechanism is settled.

## Candidate concepts

Dave's excavation/conveyor analogy prompted exploration of solids handling and moving filtration surfaces. The specific mechanisms below arose as candidate ideas in that discussion, including assistant suggestions, rather than tested outcomes.

| Candidate | Question to test | Main uncertainty to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gravity-draining basket followed by a piston, diaphragm, screw, or pneumatic bladder press | Can mechanical recovery replace manual squeezing without sacrificing yield or flavor? | Cake compaction, fines passage, cleaning, and pressed-fraction quality |
| Moving porous belt or belt filter press | Can drainage, recovery, and solids discharge be combined with less handling? | Cake formation, transfer loss, belt cleaning, and performance at current batch size |
| Screw conveyor with optional pressing section | Can wet grounds be transported and drained with fewer manual transfers? | Shear, compaction, fines release, retained liquid, and cleanability |
| Moving-bed cake filtration | Can a permeable grounds layer be maintained while handling solids continuously? | Cake stability and whether movement preserves or disrupts permeability |
| Self-cleaning screens using scraping or backwashing | Can fouling be controlled with less operator intervention? | Fines capture, product lost in cleaning, added water, and cleaning burden |
| Controlled crossflow or surface movement | Can surface loading be reduced while preserving useful solids separation? | Cake disruption, recirculation effects, and additional equipment/labor |
| Automated batch loading, drainage/pressing, and discharge | Which repeated handling operation is worth automating? | Total cycle time, reliability, sanitation, and maintenance |

A concept may serve one stage rather than replace the entire filtration sequence. Start with the measured bottleneck and the simplest feasible comparison; equipment development is not itself evidence of process improvement.

## Secondary clarification candidates

Paper, freeze-assisted clarification, capsule filters, and membrane systems remain distinct options with different research questions. Compare them against the current baseline rather than assuming all are needed in series.

The previously discussed **10 µm and 1 µm capsule options** should be recorded with manufacturer/model, media, stated rating and whether nominal or absolute, filter area, feed preparation, and operating conditions. Do not assume either rating predicts Diamond Brew performance or defines a mandatory sequence. Test individual stages and a staged configuration only where appropriate, measuring fouling, hold-up, recovery, replacement/cleaning work, and sensory effects.

**Membrane/press and spiral-cassette concepts** remain configuration ideas requiring a precise equipment description. Those labels alone do not establish membrane type, pore rating, flow geometry, or suitability. Record the actual separation mechanism and distinguish bulk solids pressing from membrane clarification before interpreting results.

For any candidate, evaluate food-contact suitability, cleanability, and manufacturer-approved operating limits. Industrial or excavation equipment is an analogy for mechanisms, not an approved food-processing solution. See [Safety](../docs/SAFETY.md).

## Measurement plan

Use traceable, comparable batches and document coffee, grind distribution, extraction conditions, feed temperature, settling/mixing history, filter area, geometry, and stage sequence.

Record:

- loading, transfer, gravity-drain, pressing, downstream filtration, discharge, and cleaning times;
- elapsed cycle time separately from active operator time; sum person-minutes if multiple people participate;
- input mass, gravity recovery, additional pressing recovery, transfer/filter hold-up, retained wet solids, and residual-liquid estimate with its method;
- final usable concentrate mass and volume, with the acceptance criteria and measurement temperature stated;
- filter ratings/materials, operating conditions, clogging, interruptions, reuse, and consumable use;
- turbidity or visual clarity, bottled sediment at defined refrigerated intervals, and standardized sensory results;
- differences between gravity, pressed, and recombined fractions;
- deviations, failed runs, uncertainty, and all missing measurements.

Calculate **hands-on person-minutes per liter of usable concentrate**, including loading, discharge, and cleaning. Report recovery and elapsed throughput alongside that number. Do not treat a measured liter as comparable if concentration or quality changed substantially; include a matched-strength comparison or an explicitly defined standardized-serving basis. Clarification trials and sediment observations do not establish microbial safety.

## Sequence and decision rule

1. Measure the existing process across independent production days to locate the labor and loss bottlenecks.
2. Test the filtration-feed/cake explanations using the existing medium before selecting hardware.
3. Compare a simple mechanical recovery aid with manual squeezing, retaining gravity and pressed fractions for assessment.
4. Evaluate secondary clarification or solids-handling concepts only when baseline measurements justify them.
5. Repeat promising results and report failure modes, cleaning burden, and sensory tradeoffs.

Predefine acceptable recovery and beverage quality, then assess whether labor falls within those constraints. Retain a stage only when its measured contribution justifies its labor and product loss. A faster drainage time alone does not establish a better overall process.
