# P1 — Hold-Time Response

## Research question

Within the current Diamond Brew temperature region, how does hold time affect concentrate recovery, measured strength, clarification behavior, and sensory character?

## Why this comes first

Time is controllable, inexpensive to vary, and central to both extraction and thermal transformation. A useful result could shorten production, improve quality, or show that the present range contains unnecessary thermal exposure.

## Primary hypothesis

Measured recovery or strength will rise rapidly and then approach a plateau, while sensory characteristics may continue to change after solids recovery slows.

## Conditions

Use at least three hold-time conditions within the current safe operating method. A practical initial comparison is:

- short: 5 minutes;
- middle: 10 minutes;
- long: 15 minutes.

Do not exceed equipment or established safe operating limits. Define precisely when the hold begins—for example, when the monitored process reaches the target condition—and apply that definition consistently.

## Design

Preferred design:

- same coffee lot and roast batch;
- same water batch;
- same grind and ratio;
- same product-container geometry and fill;
- random assignment of conditions to equivalent containers or vessel positions;
- same agitation rule unless agitation is inherently linked to hold time;
- same cooling and filtration;
- at least three independent production-day repetitions.

If conditions must be processed in separate canner runs, rotate their run order across repetitions.

## Measurements

Record:

- input coffee and water mass;
- come-up time;
- target-condition start and end;
- cooling time;
- recovered concentrate mass;
- filtration time and filter loss;
- °Brix or TDS using a documented sample-preparation method;
- pH where useful;
- dilution required to reach one common tasting strength;
- visual turbidity;
- sensory results.

## Sensory comparison

Evaluate two ways:

1. **Equal recipe dilution** — shows the combined effect of process on strength and flavor.
2. **Matched final strength** — reduces strength as an explanation for sensory differences.

Use randomized three-digit sample codes. Record attribute intensity before preference.

Suggested attributes:

- aroma intensity;
- origin-character clarity;
- roast emphasis;
- bitterness;
- astringency;
- perceived acidity;
- sweetness;
- body;
- integration;
- cooked or processed character;
- overall preference.

## Decision rule

Adopt the shortest condition that:

- produces recovery and strength within the acceptable operating range;
- is not consistently inferior in blinded sensory evaluation;
- does not create unacceptable filtration loss or variability.

If the longer hold changes flavor without improving recovery, treat it as a style choice or thermal-processing variable—not “more extraction” by default.

## Important limitations

- Pressure-canner position and run may create batch effects.
- °Brix is not automatically equivalent to coffee TDS or extraction yield.
- One coffee cannot establish a universal time.
- Sensory matching by strength may require careful dilution and recalculation.
