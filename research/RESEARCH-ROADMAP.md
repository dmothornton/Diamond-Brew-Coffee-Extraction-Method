# Diamond Brew Research Roadmap

## Purpose

This roadmap converts the scientific model into experiments that can support, constrain, or reject its proposed mechanisms.

The priorities are based on four criteria:

1. **Explanatory power** — how many competing explanations the result can distinguish.
2. **Decision value** — whether the result could change the method or its specification.
3. **Feasibility** — whether the experiment can be run with current equipment and realistic quantities.
4. **Safety and interpretability** — whether the test can be conducted without modifying pressure-safety systems or introducing uncontrolled hazards.

This is a development research program, not a substitute for an accredited laboratory, process authority, or validated food-safety study.

## Research sequence

P0 is the foundational scientific experiment because temperature defines Diamond Brew, but it requires suitable temperature-controlled pressure equipment and direct temperature measurement. P2 can be run immediately with existing concentrate. P1 and P3 can follow during normal production. Priority therefore distinguishes explanatory importance from operational sequence.

## P0 — Temperature response

See [P0-TEMPERATURE-RESPONSE.md](P0-TEMPERATURE-RESPONSE.md).

**Question:** Do extraction and sensory behavior change continuously across the high-temperature region, or is there evidence of a transition near 121 °C?

**Competing models:**

- temperature produces a largely continuous acceleration or transformation;
- a practically important nonlinear region occurs near the current target;
- apparent temperature effects are primarily differences in time, pressure, evaporation, or equipment;
- 121 °C is useful as a reproducible method convention without being a natural threshold.

**Decision:** Retain nominal 121 °C as the current defining condition while accumulating evidence about the useful operating boundary. Do not broaden the definition based on a single experiment.

## First operational research cycle

The first operational cycle should run P2, then P1 and P3. Together they address final dilution, thermal exposure, and agitation using equipment already in service.

### Common controls

For P1, P2, and P3:

- use one coffee lot and roast batch;
- use one water batch or documented water recipe;
- use the same grinder, burr set, and grind setting;
- keep coffee and water masses constant except where dilution is the tested variable;
- use the same vessel type, product-container geometry, fill level, cooling method, and filtration sequence;
- label samples with randomized codes before sensory evaluation;
- record all deviations;
- treat each pressure-cooker or production run as a possible batch effect;
- repeat the comparison on at least three separate production days before treating the result as repeatable.

One successful tasting can generate a hypothesis. It cannot establish a stable method rule.

## P1 — Hold-time response

See [P1-HOLD-TIME.md](P1-HOLD-TIME.md).

**Question:** At the present temperature region, does extending the hold materially improve recovery, or mainly change sensory character?

**Competing models:**

- extraction continues meaningfully across the full hold range;
- most accessible material is recovered early and later time mainly changes chemistry;
- apparent differences are dominated by batch variation rather than hold time.

**Decision:** Establish a default time region and identify when additional hold time has diminishing or negative returns.

## P2 — Dilution-response curve

See [P2-DILUTION-CURVE.md](P2-DILUTION-CURVE.md).

**Question:** Does a Diamond Brew have a reproducible dilution optimum rather than simply becoming weaker as water is added?

**Competing models:**

- preference tracks maximum intensity;
- dilution reduces masking and reveals a coffee-specific optimum;
- preferred dilution mostly tracks final TDS;
- service temperature or evaluator expectation explains the result.

**Decision:** Create coffee-specific serving guidance and test the claim that clarity can improve beyond approximately 1 part concentrate plus 5 parts added water by mass.

## P3 — Agitation contribution

See [P3-AGITATION.md](P3-AGITATION.md).

**Question:** Does controlled agitation materially improve recovery or repeatability, and what clarification cost does it impose?

**Competing models:**

- agitation improves slurry uniformity and extraction;
- natural convection and fine particles already provide sufficient mixing;
- agitation changes fines transfer more than dissolved-solids recovery;
- apparent improvement is caused by another correlated process difference.

**Decision:** Retain, simplify, standardize, or eliminate parts of the agitation procedure.

## Second research cycle

Run after the first cycle reveals stable measurement and sensory procedures.

### P4 — Matched-strength process comparison

Compare Diamond Brew with a lower-temperature method using the same coffee and water. Match final beverage strength through dilution or formulation, then evaluate blinded.

This cannot perfectly isolate one variable because the methods differ in temperature, time, and possibly concentration path. Its value is testing the broader claim that direct Diamond Brew extraction creates a distinguishable result at equivalent serving strength.

### P5 — Water-profile comparison

#### P5a — Extraction-water composition

Use one coffee and an otherwise fixed extraction process. Vary extraction-water composition while holding dilution water fixed. Compare randomized samples at matched final strength and record recovery as well as sensory results.

#### P5b — Dilution-water composition

Split one homogeneous concentrate and vary only dilution water, holding concentrate-to-added-water mass ratio and service temperature fixed. Begin by testing whether the reported distilled-water advantage replicates, including distilled versus both Balance and Delicate profiles with documented compositions. Document sources and recipes rather than relying on profile names.

After replication, test individual mineral additions or a planned factorial design. A crossed extraction-water × dilution-water study can test interactions once each stage is characterized. Neither stage alone identifies the responsible ions or establishes a universally preferred recipe.

### P6 — Clarification-stage fractionation

See [P6 — Filtration and Scaling](P6-FILTRATION-AND-SCALING.md) for paper-filter provenance, candidate mechanical/continuous systems, secondary clarification options, and hands-on labor measurement.

Retain samples after each stage:

1. pre-filtration slurry sample where safe and practical;
2. cloth-filtered extract;
3. paper-filtered extract;
4. freeze-clarified or membrane-treated extract, when applicable.

Measure mass recovery, strength, visible turbidity, filtration time, and sensory change. This can identify whether a step adds enough value to justify its product loss and labor.

#### Filtration-feed comparison

Compare whole slurry, decanted liquid, and decanted liquid passed through a controlled grounds precoat using matched cloth area, geometry, temperature, and handling. Record feed mass and particle/settling history. Account for liquid and solutes introduced by the precoat. Normalize throughput to feed quantity and cloth area.

Measure gravity-drain time and recovered mass separately from pressing time and additional recovery; record wet retained solids, residual-liquid estimate and method, and downstream paper/capsule filtration time, recovery, and turbidity. Use a mass balance and blind sensory assessment to evaluate labor, loss, and quality together.

If cake formation is important, a suitable precoat should improve decanted-liquid filtration. If feed particle distribution dominates, the effect may depend more strongly on settling and fines content. A null precoat result alone would not rule out cake effects unless precoat structure is characterized.

### P7 — Resting time series

Split one homogeneous filtered batch into identical containers and evaluate at defined refrigerated intervals, such as day 0, day 1, day 3, and day 7.

Include both undisturbed and standardized-remixed samples where practical. This helps distinguish settling from irreversible chemical or colloidal change. It does not determine microbial safety.

## Deferred or externally supported research

### P9 — Pressure, closure, and volatile recovery

A clean pressure-versus-temperature comparison is difficult because pressure, boiling, evaporation, and vessel closure are coupled in current equipment.

Do not alter, obstruct, defeat, or improvise pressure-relief systems. Do not deliberately operate a pressure vessel in an unapproved vented state. A rigorous pressure or volatile-capture experiment should use equipment designed for controlled pressure, temperature, headspace sampling, and safe vent management.

#### Connected chambers and condensate return

The October 15 [connected-chamber proposal](P6-FILTRATION-AND-SCALING.md) couples extraction headspace with subsequent separation. Test volatile capture independently of transfer-loss and filtration claims. Compare conventional handling, connected handling without deliberate condensate return, and quantified condensate return where a suitably engineered apparatus permits these controls. Match or document extraction conditions, headspace, cooling, transfer time, final mass/strength, and oxygen exposure.

Measure condensate mass, collection temperature/time, location, storage, and returned fraction; retain samples for qualified chemical analysis and blinded sensory assessment. Include a water-addition control matched to condensate mass to distinguish dilution from condensate composition. Avoid double-counting internally redistributed condensate as net product recovery.

Competing explanations include greater volatile retention, altered volatile partitioning without net recovery, undesirable compounds returned with condensate, and sensory effects caused mainly by thermal history, headspace, or final dilution. Neither condensation nor closed handling establishes improved aroma or microbial safety. Apply the qualified apparatus and operating limits in P6 before any experiment.

### Chemical characterization

Useful outside measurements could include:

- calibrated coffee TDS after validated sample preparation;
- targeted acids and chlorogenic-acid products;
- caffeine;
- volatile profiling;
- particle-size and turbidity analysis;
- lipid or diterpene content;
- dissolved oxygen where relevant.

#### Orchard successive-extraction study — analysis pending

Two Orchard samples, representing first and second extraction, were supplied for external chemical characterization and caffeine measurement. No results are currently reported. Prepare a separate experimental record once batch and sample details are assembled; do not invent missing conditions or promote anticipated findings into the evidence register.

Compare three models: preferential early recovery of caffeine/readily soluble material, changed relative composition in later extract, and differences explained mainly by concentration. Report concentration with units, recovered extract mass, total recovered analyte mass, and analyte recovery per original dry-coffee mass. Relative composition requires a stated normalization basis; incomplete mass balances and differences in preparation or storage limit interpretation. One sample pair is exploratory, even if laboratory measurements are precise.

### P10 — Shelf life and post-fill processing

Quality-life observations and microbial safety must remain separate. Shelf-stability work should be designed with a qualified process authority or food laboratory around the final formulation, container, filling process, and storage condition.

Post-fill thermal processing is an exploratory preservation direction, separate from defining extraction. Discuss product, packaging, equipment, heat penetration, and controls with qualified specialists before selecting a protocol or incorporating it into commercial production.

Evaluate microbial reduction, sensory change, and possible acceleration of refrigerated-resting changes as separate hypotheses with separate measurements. Prior brewing at nominally 121 °C for 30 minutes does not validate reheating filtered concentrate in sealed bottles. Vessel temperature and elapsed hold do not establish cold-spot product exposure, closure integrity, shelf stability, or commercial sterility. See [Safety](../docs/SAFETY.md).

## Evidence thresholds

Use the following working categories:

| Level | Minimum basis | Permitted conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exploratory | One controlled comparison | Worth repeating |
| Repeated internal observation | At least three independent production-day comparisons with the same directional result | Provisional internal method guidance |
| Strong internal evidence | Repeated result across coffees or independent operators with documented measurements and blinded sensory work | Public empirical claim with limitations |
| External validation | Qualified independent analysis or replicated outside work | Higher-confidence scientific or safety claim within the tested scope |

## Reporting

Every completed experiment should produce:

- a completed experiment record;
- raw measurements;
- sample codes and sensory responses;
- photographs where useful;
- a short conclusion;
- alternative explanations;
- the operational decision taken;
- a link from the evidence register.

Null and negative results should be retained.
