# Safety

## Pressure and heat

Diamond Brew uses equipment capable of producing severe burns, vessel rupture, or other injury. Users must:

- operate equipment only within manufacturer specifications;
- inspect seals, valves, vents, gauges, and safety devices;
- never obstruct pressure-release or safety systems;
- allow safe cooling and depressurization before opening;
- use heat- and pressure-compatible vessels and closures;
- obtain appropriate training for the specific equipment.

This repository does not supersede equipment manuals, workplace requirements, or professional training.

## Food-contact suitability

Equipment marketed for laboratory, medical, industrial, or sterilization use is not automatically suitable or approved for food production. Food-contact materials, cleanability, chemical compatibility, drainage, and regulatory acceptance must be evaluated for the actual jurisdiction and use.

## Shelf stability

Reaching an indicated vessel temperature near 121 °C does not by itself establish that a packaged product is shelf stable or commercially sterile. Safety depends on factors that may include:

- cold-spot product temperature and time;
- come-up and cooling behavior;
- container size, geometry, headspace, and closure;
- formulation, pH, and water activity;
- contamination after processing;
- filling sequence and package integrity;
- storage and distribution conditions.

A process intended to create an ambient shelf-stable low-acid product generally requires specialized regulatory review and a validated scheduled process. Refrigerated storage does not remove the need for sanitation, time-temperature control, shelf-life definition, and applicable licensing.

## Research versus sale

Experimental measurements and sensory observations must not be represented as regulatory validation. Product intended for sale should be produced only under the relevant permits, approved facility conditions, labeling rules, and validated controls.

## Repository limitation

Nothing in this repository is medical, engineering, or food-safety certification. Users are responsible for qualified review and compliance in their jurisdiction.
