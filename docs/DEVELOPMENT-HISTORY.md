# Development History

This document records the public development arc of Diamond Brew. It establishes provenance and changing ideas; it should not be treated as experimental proof.

## Origins

Diamond Brew emerged from David Maurice Thornton's practical experimentation with concentrated coffee extraction and the goal of expressing specialty coffee through a service-ready concentrate.

The name reflects two related ideas:

- technically, the process combines heat and a pressurized environment;
- symbolically, it aims to reveal latent value in a coffee—the potential within the bean rather than intensity for its own sake.

The name is a metaphor, not a scientific claim that coffee undergoes a diamond-like transformation.

## Initial public repository — December 2024

The first public repository described a compact method based on:

- very fine coffee;
- adjusted brewing water;
- approximate 1:3 and 1:4 coffee-to-water ratios;
- processing near 121 °C;
- a 15-minute hold;
- cooling, filtration, and optional freezing;
- dilution for final service.

That version established an early public record but treated several hypotheses as settled and compressed the method into a single recipe.

## Operational development

Subsequent development moved Diamond Brew from a recipe concept toward an operating system used in live beverage service through Sweet Caffeine.

Important shifts included:

- treating water composition as a coffee-specific design variable;
- developing Balance, Delicate, High Aroma, and Expressive water-profile families;
- widening the process-time envelope rather than assuming one fixed hold;
- using controlled agitation as an active process variable;
- moving from a single filter description to staged cloth and paper filtration;
- exploring freeze-assisted clarification and membrane filtration;
- recognizing dilution as part of sensory composition;
- observing that refrigerated rest can change perceived integration;
- recording batch strength, recovery, pH, water activity, and other measurements;
- separating refrigerated quality observations from shelf-stability claims.

## Current phase — 2026

Diamond Brew is now being developed simultaneously as:

- a café and mobile-service beverage system;
- a concentrated retail format;
- a potential wholesale replacement or enhancement for drip and cold-brew programs;
- a coffee-specific development service in collaboration with roasters and clients;
- a structured research program.

The commercial program includes small-format servings and larger multi-serving formats under evaluation. Those applications create new requirements for repeatability, capacity, packaging, sanitation, regulatory compliance, and validated shelf life.

## Evolution of the scientific model

The early explanation emphasized “pressure and heat.” The present model is more cautious and more predictive.

Pressure clearly enables liquid-water processing near 121 °C, but the distinctive result may arise from the interaction of:

- fine-particle mass transfer;
- elevated-temperature chemistry;
- high slurry concentration;
- closed-vessel and headspace behavior;
- water composition;
- agitation;
- selective clarification;
- resting;
- final dilution.

The unique contribution of each variable has not yet been isolated.

## Documentation principle

Diamond Brew will be documented as an evolving body of work. Historical claims will be retained where useful, but later evidence may revise them. Provenance does not require pretending that early explanations were complete; it requires making the evolution visible.
