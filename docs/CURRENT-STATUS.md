# Current Status — November 1, 2026

Research evidence cutoff for this update: October 31, 2026. This update consolidates the October 19–25 and October 26–November 1 reviews; it does not imply new laboratory results. Operational event details require separate verification against the latest owner-approved plan.

Diamond Brew has advanced from a compact concept description into an actively used and researched coffee brewing methodology. Sweet Caffeine uses live beverage service to highlight the method, while retail, wholesale, and collaborative formats provide additional contexts for evaluating it.

## Relatively stable elements

The following elements recur consistently enough to form the current method envelope:

- very fine grinding;
- a closed vessel near 121 °C;
- concentrated coffee-to-water ratios by mass, commonly near 1:3 or 1:4;
- a process hold within approximately 5–15 minutes;
- deliberate agitation;
- coffee-specific water selection;
- staged filtration and clarification;
- final beverage formulation by dilution.

“Stable” means routinely used in development. It does not mean independently validated or fully optimized.

## Active development areas

- higher-concentration formulations;
- coffee-specific selection of water profile and brew ratio;
- repeatable concentrate recovery and extraction-yield measurement;
- scaling vessel geometry and batch capacity;
- particle removal without stripping desirable oils or texture;
- capsule and membrane filtration;
- mechanical recovery and solids-handling concepts evaluated against hands-on labor, recovery, and beverage quality;
- resting and refrigerated storage behavior;
- small-format and multi-serving packaging;
- retail and wholesale applications;
- analytical and microbial shelf-life testing.

## Method and applications

Diamond Brew is the brewing methodology. Concentrates, packaged products, café service, wholesale programs, and client-specific formulations are applications of that methodology.

Public documentation will focus on the scientific model, observations, method development, and reproducible research. Every published experiment should disclose the coffee, water recipe, equipment, geometry, process conditions, measurements, and other variables needed for independent repetition. Unpublished client formulations, production economics, and commercial workflows that are not part of a published experiment need not be included.

## Near-term research priorities

1. Standardize mass-balance and extraction measurements.
2. Establish a controlled experiment for time, temperature, and agitation.
3. Quantify the relationship between concentrate strength and preferred dilution.
4. Compare clarification sequences using recovery, turbidity, and sensory outcomes.
5. Separate refrigerated quality-life testing from microbial shelf-life validation.
6. Develop a traceable batch and sample-naming system.

## October research checkpoint

- Extraction water and dilution/service water are being treated as separate formulation variables; the reported distilled dilution-water advantage remains preliminary.
- Whole-slurry muslin filtration was reported to drain more readily than decanted liquid. Filter-cake formation and particle-distribution effects remain competing, overlapping explanations. Controlled recovery and labor comparisons are planned.
- Orchard samples were supplied for external chemical and caffeine analysis. An October 22 collaborator update reported sample loss and laboratory closure; the study is blocked. Surviving sample identities, usability, complete metadata, and testing date remain unresolved. No analytical findings are established.
- Fine-interval dilution trials and independent professional sensory evaluation are proposed; no universal optimum is established.
- Post-fill thermal processing is exploratory preservation research, not a validated production or shelf-stability control.

These developments expand the research program without changing the nominal 121 °C extraction criterion, usual 5–15 minute developmental hold range, or approximate 1:3/1:4 starting ratios. Service doses such as 30 mL or 40 mL remain application choices rather than defining method parameters.
