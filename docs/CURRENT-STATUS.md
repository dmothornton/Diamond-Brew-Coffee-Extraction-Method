# Current Status — September 2026

Diamond Brew has advanced from a compact concept description into an actively used and researched coffee brewing methodology. Sweet Caffeine uses live beverage service to highlight the method, while retail, wholesale, and collaborative formats provide additional contexts for evaluating it.

## Relatively stable elements

The following elements recur consistently enough to form the current method envelope:

- very fine grinding;
- a closed vessel near 121 °C;
- concentrated brewing ratios, commonly near 1:3 or 1:4;
- a process hold within approximately 5–15 minutes;
- deliberate agitation;
- coffee-specific water selection;
- staged filtration and clarification;
- final beverage formulation by dilution.

“Stable” means routinely used in development. It does not mean independently validated or fully optimized.

## Active development areas

- higher-concentration formulations;
- coffee-specific selection of water profile and brew ratio;
- repeatable concentrate recovery and extraction-yield measurement;
- scaling vessel geometry and batch capacity;
- particle removal without stripping desirable oils or texture;
- capsule and membrane filtration;
- resting and refrigerated storage behavior;
- small-format and multi-serving packaging;
- retail and wholesale applications;
- analytical and microbial shelf-life testing.

## Method and applications

Diamond Brew is the brewing methodology. Concentrates, packaged products, café service, wholesale programs, and client-specific formulations are applications of that methodology.

Public documentation will focus on the scientific model, selected observations, method development, and reproducible research practices. Exact client formulations, detailed production SOPs, water recipes, production economics, and unannounced commercial specifications are not necessarily included.

## Near-term research priorities

1. Standardize mass-balance and extraction measurements.
2. Establish a controlled experiment for time, temperature, and agitation.
3. Quantify the relationship between concentrate strength and preferred dilution.
4. Compare clarification sequences using recovery, turbidity, and sensory outcomes.
5. Separate refrigerated quality-life testing from microbial shelf-life validation.
6. Develop a traceable batch and sample-naming system.
