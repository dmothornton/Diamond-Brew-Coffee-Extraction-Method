# Evidence and Limitations

This register prevents practical observations, scientific interpretations, and commercial goals from being treated as equivalent evidence.

## Current evidence register

| Statement | Classification | Confidence | What would strengthen it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The process produces a concentrated coffee extract suitable for dilution | Repeated observation | High | Standardized mass balance and solids measurement |
| Approximate 1:3 and 1:4 ratios are useful starting regions | Empirical observation | Moderate | Multi-coffee controlled comparison |
| Water profile changes sensory expression | Repeated observation plus established external science | Moderate-to-high | Blind replicated trials |
| Dilution near 1:4–1:5 is often useful | Empirical observation | Moderate | Structured dilution curves by coffee |
| Greater dilution can improve perceived clarity | Observation | Low-to-moderate | Blinded sensory panels |
| Resting can improve perceived integration | Observation | Low | Time-series controls and analytical measurements |
| Pressure itself materially improves extraction | Hypothesis | Low | Equal-temperature pressure-control experiment |
| Approximate extraction yield is around 10% | Preliminary estimate | Low | Standardized TDS and retained-liquid accounting |
| A measured °Brix value represents extraction yield | Unsupported shortcut | Low | Coffee-specific refractometry method and mass balance |
| The process produces a shelf-stable concentrate | Not established | Very low | Validated formulation, process authority review, challenge/shelf-life testing |
| Processing at 121 °C makes every package commercially sterile | Not established | Very low | Validated scheduled process in the final package and production system |
| Refrigerated sensory stability establishes microbial safety | Unsupported inference | High confidence that the inference is invalid | Separate microbial and quality testing |

## Evidence traceability

As the experiment record grows, claims should link to one or more evidence sources:

- experiment or batch identifiers;
- instrument records;
- blind sensory trials;
- repeated service observations;
- external primary literature;
- qualified laboratory or process-authority findings.

The strength of a claim should be revised when new evidence supports, limits, or contradicts it.

## Measurement cautions

### °Brix

A refractometer reading can be useful for batch comparison, but °Brix is calibrated around sucrose solutions and is not automatically equivalent to coffee total dissolved solids. Oils, suspended material, temperature, and instrument calibration can affect readings.

### Extraction yield

Extraction yield requires a defined measurement method. A reliable estimate needs, at minimum, beverage mass, an appropriate dissolved-solids measurement, and accounting for retained liquid and losses. Results from concentrated, oily, or particle-rich samples may require dilution or additional sample preparation.

### pH and water activity

pH and water activity answer different questions. Neither should be treated as a universal stand-alone guarantee of shelf stability. Targets are formulation goals until the final product and process have been evaluated by qualified food-safety professionals.

## Known limitations

- Most sensory findings arise from development practice rather than blinded replicated trials.
- Roast level and coffee identity are often changed alongside other variables.
- Vessel geometry, batch size, agitation, and cooling may be confounded.
- Current instruments may be insufficient for precise compositional claims.
- Aroma retention, degradation, and transformation have not been chemically characterized.
- Shelf-life goals have not yet been validated.
- The method is evolving, so historical batches may not be directly comparable.

## Research principle

Future documentation should record disconfirming evidence and failed trials, not only successful recipes. A useful model must predict when Diamond Brew will not work or when a simpler process will perform equally well.
