# Current Public Method Envelope

**Status:** Developmental  
**Last revised:** September 2026

This document describes the present public process envelope for Diamond Brew. It is not a validated food-manufacturing process, a universal recipe, or the complete commercial production specification.

## 1. Inputs

### Coffee

Diamond Brew has been developed across multiple roast levels and origins. Coffee selection affects the useful brew ratio, water profile, processing time, clarification behavior, and final dilution.

### Water

Water chemistry is treated as an active formulation variable. Current development uses several profile families—Balance, Delicate, High Aroma, and Expressive—to explore how mineral composition changes extraction and sensory expression.

Exact formulations are outside the current public specification. The profile names describe development tools, not standardized industry water specifications.

## 2. Particle preparation

Coffee is ground approximately Turkish-fine or finer. At this particle size:

- available surface area is high;
- extraction can proceed rapidly;
- fines management becomes a central process constraint;
- grinder condition and particle distribution may affect repeatability.

The importance of the complete particle-size distribution, rather than nominal grind setting alone, remains under study.

## 3. Brew ratio

Development has often begun near:

- 1:3 coffee-to-water by mass for more developed roasts;
- 1:4 coffee-to-water by mass for lighter roasts.

These are empirical starting regions rather than roast-based rules. Roast development may be acting as a proxy for solubility, bean structure, flavor intensity, degradation sensitivity, filtration behavior, or the desired serving concentration.

## 4. Thermal-pressure processing

The closed vessel is brought to approximately 121 °C, with pressure sufficient to maintain liquid water at the target temperature. Current holds commonly fall within approximately 5–15 minutes.

The process is better described as **elevated-temperature extraction in a closed pressurized vessel** than simply “high-pressure brewing.” The current evidence does not isolate pressure as the dominant extraction variable.

## 5. Agitation

Controlled agitation may be applied during processing to redistribute the dense coffee-water slurry and reduce local extraction differences. Timing and intensity remain active development variables.

## 6. Cooling and separation

The vessel is cooled and depressurized according to the equipment manufacturer's instructions before opening.

Current clarification may include:

1. coarse filtration through food-suitable cloth;
2. secondary paper filtration;
3. optional freeze-assisted clarification or separation.

The aim is to remove objectionable particles while retaining selected oils and colloidal material where they contribute positively to texture and flavor. “Freeze clarification” should not be assumed to mean freeze concentration.

## 7. Resting and storage

Refrigerated concentrate has shown sensory change during rest, sometimes perceived as improved integration. The mechanism has not been established. Possible contributors include gas release, particle settling, oxidation, colloidal change, and changes in aroma partitioning.

No storage observation in this repository establishes shelf stability or commercial safety. Unvalidated batches should remain under appropriately controlled refrigeration and applicable food-safety procedures.

## 8. Dilution and service

Final dilution is part of the method rather than an afterthought. Development servings commonly begin around 1 part concentrate to 4–5 parts water, then adjust for coffee, desired strength, service temperature, and beverage application.

Greater dilution has sometimes produced improved clarity even when the concentrate initially appeared very strong. This is an observation requiring structured sensory testing.

## 9. Dialing-in framework

A public description of the development sequence is:

1. Choose an initial brew ratio based on the coffee and intended application.
2. Choose a starting water profile from the current profile families.
3. Process a reference sample under a documented standard condition.
4. Evaluate the concentrate at multiple controlled dilutions.
5. Identify the principal failure mode or opportunity—for example muted origin character, excessive roast emphasis, weak structure, muddled flavor separation, or filtration loss.
6. Adjust one primary variable, such as water profile, ratio, hold time, agitation, clarification, or serving dilution.
7. Repeat using traceable batch and sample identifiers.

This framework describes the logic of development without prescribing a universal formulation.

## 10. Measurements under development

Useful batch measurements include:

- coffee and water input mass;
- recovered concentrate mass;
- process time and temperature;
- agitation schedule;
- °Brix or total dissolved solids;
- pH;
- filtration loss;
- serving dilution;
- structured sensory results;
- storage time and temperature.

Extraction yield estimates remain provisional until measurements and methods are standardized.
