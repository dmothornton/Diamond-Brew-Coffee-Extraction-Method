# Scientific Bibliography

This bibliography supports the scientific model described in this repository. It prioritizes primary research. Inclusion does not imply that a paper directly tested Diamond Brew.

Diamond Brew combines conditions that are not represented by a single published study located in this initial review: a highly concentrated, very-fine coffee slurry processed in a closed vessel near 121 °C, followed by staged clarification and dilution. The literature below supports component mechanisms or identifies important limitations on inference.

## Extraction physics and particle-scale transport

### [1] Moroney et al. (2015)

Moroney, K. M., Lee, W. T., O’Brien, S. B. G., Suijver, F., & Marra, J. “Modelling of coffee extraction during brewing using multiscale methods: An experimentally validated model.” *Chemical Engineering Science*, 137, 216–234.

https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ces.2015.06.003

**Relevance:** Experimentally modeled coffee extraction as rapid recovery from damaged cells near particle surfaces followed by slower transport from intact internal cells. This supports treating particle structure and diffusion distance as central variables.

**Boundary:** Experiments were conducted around 90 °C in dilute stirred suspensions and packed beds, not under Diamond Brew conditions.

### [2] Moroney et al. (2017)

Moroney, K. M., Lee, W. T., O’Brien, S. B. G., Suijver, F., & Marra, J. “Coffee extraction kinetics in a well mixed system.” *Journal of Mathematics in Industry*, 7, Article 3.

https://doi.org/10.1186/s13362-016-0024-6

**Relevance:** Examined extraction kinetics in a well-mixed system and provides a useful starting model for separating fast surface extraction from slower intraparticle transport.

**Boundary:** “Well mixed” does not establish that Diamond Brew agitation is optimal, nor does it cover its high slurry concentration and temperature.

### [3] Cameron et al. (2020)

Cameron, M. I., Morisco, D., Hofstetter, D., Uman, E., Wilkinson, J., Kennedy, Z. C., Fontenot, S. A., Lee, W. T., Hendon, C. H., & Foster, J. “Systematically improving espresso: Insights from mathematical modeling and experiment.” *Matter*, 2(3), 631–648.

https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matt.2019.12.019

**Relevance:** Demonstrated that grind, flow, bed behavior, and extraction uniformity interact nonlinearly. It supports caution against equating finer grinding automatically with more uniform or preferable extraction.

**Boundary:** Espresso is a pressure-driven porous bed, whereas Diamond Brew is primarily a concentrated slurry process.

## Temperature, strength, and sensory interpretation

### [4] Batali, Ristenpart, and Guinard (2020)

Batali, M. E., Ristenpart, W. D., & Guinard, J.-X. “Brew temperature, at fixed brew strength and extraction, has little impact on the sensory profile of drip brew coffee.” *Scientific Reports*, 10, 16450.

https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-73341-4

**Relevance:** Within the tested 87–93 °C range, brew temperature contributed little to sensory differences when brew strength and extraction were held fixed. This is important counterevidence to simplistic claims that hotter brewing necessarily produces a distinct or superior sensory result.

**Boundary:** The study did not test temperatures near 121 °C, concentrated slurry conditions, a closed vessel, or long thermal holds. Its result therefore constrains but does not resolve the Diamond Brew temperature hypothesis.

### [5] Batali et al. (2020)

Batali, M. E., Frost, S. C., Lebrilla, C. B., Ristenpart, W. D., & Guinard, J.-X. “Sensory and monosaccharide analysis of drip brew coffee fractions versus brewing time.” *Journal of the Science of Food and Agriculture*, 100(7), 2953–2962.

https://doi.org/10.1002/jsfa.10323

**Relevance:** Different fractions of a drip brew had different sensory profiles; several perceived attributes did not track total dissolved solids monotonically. This supports treating composition, masking, and concentration as distinct from a simple “more extraction equals more flavor” model.

**Boundary:** Fractionated drip brewing is not equivalent to concentrated closed-vessel extraction or post-brew dilution.

## Water composition

### [6] Hendon, Colonna-Dashwood, and Colonna-Dashwood (2014)

Hendon, C. H., Colonna-Dashwood, L., & Colonna-Dashwood, M. “The role of dissolved cations in coffee extraction.” *Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry*, 62(21), 4947–4950.

https://doi.org/10.1021/jf501687c

**Relevance:** Modeled interactions between dissolved cations and selected coffee flavor compounds, supporting the premise that water composition can change extraction behavior and perceived flavor balance.

**Boundary:** The work is not a direct validation of Diamond Brew’s named water profiles or of universal sensory preferences.

## Aroma extraction and volatile behavior

### [7] Mestdagh et al. (2014)

Mestdagh, F., Davidek, T., Chaumonteuil, M., Folmer, B., & Blank, I. “The kinetics of coffee aroma extraction.” *Food Research International*, 63, 271–274.

https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2014.03.011

**Relevance:** Demonstrated that coffee aroma compounds have compound-specific extraction kinetics. This supports avoiding the assumption that soluble solids and aroma are recovered in identical proportions.

**Boundary:** It does not test Diamond Brew’s closed-vessel headspace, condensation, or high-temperature hold.

### [8] Sánchez López et al. (2016)

Sánchez López, J. A., Wellinger, M., Gloess, A. N., Zimmermann, R., & Yeretzian, C. “Extraction kinetics of coffee aroma compounds using a semi-automatic machine: On-line analysis by PTR-ToF-MS.” *International Journal of Mass Spectrometry*, 401, 22–30.

https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijms.2016.02.015

**Relevance:** Used real-time mass spectrometry to show time-dependent extraction of volatile compounds during coffee preparation. This supports direct measurement of aroma behavior rather than inferring it from total solids.

**Boundary:** Semi-automatic coffee-machine extraction differs materially from a closed, pressurized slurry.

## Evidence gaps identified by this review

The following Diamond Brew questions remain largely unsupported by direct published evidence:

- the incremental effect of pressure when temperature and liquid state are controlled;
- volatile retention, degradation, and redistribution in the specific closed-vessel process;
- chemical changes caused by holding roasted coffee near 121 °C;
- extraction behavior at Diamond Brew’s high coffee-to-water ratios;
- the effects of its agitation schedule;
- the composition of material removed or retained by its clarification sequence;
- the mechanism behind sensory changes during refrigerated rest;
- whether dilution reveals attributes through reduced masking, changed volatility, lower viscosity, or another mechanism.

These gaps should be treated as the Diamond Brew research program—not filled with certainty borrowed from adjacent studies.
